# The compute-module kit: Raspberry Pi CM5 carrier, radios on native buses, three-module option, 4 September 2026

Scope: the owner asked (4 Sep 2026, night) whether a Raspberry Pi Compute Module 5 on a carrier of our own, with the module's USB, GPIO, I2C, SPI, UART, I2S, DSI and 5 V landing on our PCB and no USB socket or plug between the compute and the kit, would improve the field kit substantially; which peripherals can leave USB for a documented native interface; and what it takes to host three modules on one carrier for customers who want redundancy (Docker Swarm or k3s, the CubeOS HAL owning the hardware). Five read-only explorations answered it from the makers' documents and both software repositories. The rulings and the design are in appendix 32.35; this note carries the figures and their sources. Nothing described here has been built.

## 1. What V2 does today

- Two USB hubs: FE1.1s on B12 (SDR bay, ZigBee, LoRa XIAO or T-Beam, T-Call; RockBLOCK is a power-only channel) and USB2517I on A19 (WiFi, GPS, mezzanine codec, an unused mezzanine UART channel, the wall host port). The Pi reaches them through two USB-C receptacles on B12 and two short cables into its USB-A ports; A19's upstream pair rides the 14-way `J_AB1` ribbon (`gen_sch_b.py` lines 146 to 161, 207; `gen_sch_a.py` 207 to 251).
- Count: nine populated USB connectors, seven cables or ribbons, fifteen mating pairs between the Pi and its devices, sixteen with the Pi's power plug (`J_5V_PI` lead into the Pi's USB-C with a 10k Rp, `gen_sch_b.py` 128). Nine USB devices enumerate.
- The 40-pin ribbon `J_GPIO1` carries I2C, SPI0, two UARTs, PWM and eleven GPIO lines (`gen_sch_b.py` 140 to 144). The display is DSI; the Pi boots from a microSD.
- The T-Call chain: Pi, USB, CH9102, ESP32 passthrough firmware, UART, A7670E; the modem's own USB is on two test points of the LilyGO board only (its V1.0 schematic, TP2 and TP3), and the ESP32 owns the modem UART.
- V1 evidence that USB is the weak point: the 600 mA USB cap until `PSU_MAX_CURRENT=5000` (`v1/BUILD.md` 214), hub power and a ganged VBUS that cannot clear a wedged XIAO (MESHSAT-781), USB autosuspend breaking the T-Call's CDC path, DTR pulses resetting the ESP32, re-enumeration moving ttyACM paths, the AIOC needing a USB reset before Direwolf can open it (`scripts/direwolf-preflight.sh`), MT7921U dongles dropping off the bus, "USB cables are the usual culprit" in the bridge README.
- The bridge (`~/gitlab/products/cubeos/meshsat`) uses no gpsd, no ModemManager and no zigbee2mqtt: it speaks NMEA, AT, ZNP and Meshtastic framing itself over serial ports found by VID:PID with a probe cascade (`internal/transport/device_supervisor.go`), scans `ttyUSB*`, `ttyACM*` and `ttyAMA*`, bundles Direwolf driven through ALSA with PTT on a CDC RTS line, and already reaches Meshtastic and Iridium over the HAL's REST and SSE (`hal_mesh.go`, `hal_sat.go`).

## 2. The Compute Module 5 (official documents in `vendor/cm5/`)

Datasheet release 3 of 8 June 2026 (RP-008180-DS-7), product brief September 2026, CM5IO datasheet and its KiCad design files RP-008099-DD, the documentation sources. Page numbers are the datasheet's PDF pages.

- Module 55 x 40 x 4.7 mm, PCB 1.24 mm, four M2.5 holes inset 3.5 mm (section 4.1.1, p. 17). Operating range -20 to +85 C, wireless best to +75 C; MTBF 143 000 h ground benign, 16 000 h ground mobile (section 4.4, p. 28). Production to at least January 2036.
- Two 100-pin connectors; carrier side Amphenol `10164227-1001A1RLF` (1.5 mm stack, no clearance under the module) or `10164227-1004A1RLF` (4.0 mm stack, 2.5 mm clearance); the second connector may be omitted at the cost of stability (section 4.2.1, p. 23). The module-side part, the pitch (0.4 mm per Amphenol) and the mating cycles are not in the Raspberry Pi documents. The CM5IO uses the 1.5 mm part on both connectors; its footprint `Raspberry-Pi-5-Compute-Module.kicad_mod` carries both connectors and the outline.
- Signals (Table 4, pp. 18 to 23): two USB 3.0 ports each with its own USB 2.0 pair (pins 134/136 and 163/165), one dedicated USB 2.0 OTG pair (103/105, `USB_OTG_ID` 101; rpiboot), so three USB 2.0 pairs and no hub on the module; PCIe Gen 2 x1; two 4-lane MIPI ports, each DSI or CSI (MIPI0 pins 115 to 141, MIPI1 175 to 196); two HDMI 2.0; Ethernet PHY BCM54210PE on the module with four MDI pairs (3 to 12) and no RGMII; SDIO for the Lite variant; 28 GPIO (GPIO0 to 27) with `GPIO_VREF` (78) to `CM5_3.3V` or `CM5_1.8V`; `SDA0`/`SCL0` (82/80) and `ID_SD`/`ID_SC` (36/35); 5 V input on pins 77, 79, 81, 83, 85, 87 at 4.75 to 5.25 V; `CM5_3.3V` (84/86) and `CM5_1.8V` (88/90) outputs 600 mA each; `VBAT` 76; `PWR_Button` 92; `PMIC_Enable` 99; `nRPIBOOT` 93; `EEPROM_nWP` 20; `Fan_PWM` 19 (open drain) and `Fan_Tacho` 16; `VBUS_EN` 111; `LED_nACT` 21, `LED_nPWR` 95; `WL_nDisable` 89, `BT_nDisable` 91; `CC1`/`CC2` 94/96. RP1 peripherals through the GPIO alt functions: four I2C, six SPI (SPI4 slave), five UART, two I2S (GPIO 18 to 21; one clock producer or one consumer, no MCLK, no TDM per the I2S white paper RP-009699-WP-1), four PWM, DPI.
- Power: design the supply for 5 V at 2.5 A (appendix B.3, p. 36); idle 400 mA, operating 900 mA (Table 9, p. 27); the 5 A figure is a USB-C PD negotiation on `CC1`/`CC2` only. The module's rails must not be back-fed when it is off, and nothing on `CM5_3.3V` may outlive `CM5_1.8V` (section 4.2.1).
- Routing: USB 2.0, USB 3.0 and PCIe at 90 ohm differential, MIPI, HDMI and Ethernet at 100 ohm, intra-pair within 0.15 mm (0.1 for USB 3.0), USB 2.0 pairs not P/N swappable (sections 2.2 to 2.5). The CM5IO is a four-layer 1.6 mm board with a 90 um outer dielectric; its net classes: `90R` 0.147/0.253 mm for USB and PCIe, `100R` 0.127/0.253 mm for MIPI and Ethernet, vias 0.45/0.2 (from `CM5IO.kicad_pro`). On JLCPCB's 7628 four-layer stack the same targets are about 0.20/0.15 mm for 90 ohm and 0.17/0.15 mm for 100 ohm, to be confirmed with JLC's calculator at upload; A19 and B12 already route their USB pairs that way.
- ESD: the CM5 removed the CM4's on-module ESD on HDMI, I2C, HPD and CEC (appendix B.1.2, p. 36); every external pair gets the carrier's own array (the CM5IO fits TPD4EUSB30).
- Wireless: CYW43455, 802.11ac and Bluetooth 5, an on-module switch between the PCB antenna and a U.FL selected at boot by `dtparam=ant1` or `ant2` (section 2.1, p. 7); PCB antenna keep-out 10 mm and a 6.5 x 11 mm ground cut-out (section 4.1.2, p. 18); the certified antenna kit (RP-008179-DS) is the CM4 kit, 2 dBi SMA whip on a 205 mm U.FL lead, USD 5; a third-party antenna needs its own certification.
- Thermal: less passive sinking than the Pi 5, throttling below 85 C SoC (section 4.4, p. 28); the CM5 Cooler is a passive 41 x 56 x 12.7 mm block with a cut-out over the antenna (documentation page; verify the height on the part); a fan on `Fan_PWM` needs the `74LVC1G07` gate or `VBUS_EN` to stop after shutdown (section 2.11, p. 13; the CM5IO does this).
- Software: the Pi 5 images and RP1 stack; Raspberry Pi OS is the documented OS (Ubuntu appears only as an rpiboot host); a Compute Module does not auto-detect a DSI display, the carrier's hardware is declared in an overlay (`cm-peri-sw-guide`); the eMMC is flashed with `nRPIBOOT` low and `rpiboot` on a host over the dedicated USB 2.0 pair (`cm-emmc-flashing`). Touch Display 2 on a CM: a 22-pin 0.5 mm FPC (Hirose FH12-22S-0.5SH on the CM5IO) with the "Standard-Mini" 22-to-15 cable, default port CAM/DISP 1 (MIPI1, whose control I2C is `ID_SD`/`ID_SC`), 5 V by the 2-wire lead.
- Prices: 8 GB wireless with 32 or 64 GB eMMC USD 205 list (product brief p. 5; 32 and 64 GB cost the same); Reichelt EUR 216.70 for the 32 GB variant in stock on 4 Sep; IO board about EUR 22; cooler about EUR 5.

## 4. Three modules on one carrier (documents in `vendor/cluster/`)

- No three-module CM5 carrier exists; four is the market's number (DeskPi Super4C, Turing Pi 2.5, Cerebro, Sipeed NanoCluster in practice). The Super4C (CM5 only, four modules, USD 219) has no switch on the board: each module's PHY goes to an RJ45; four independent 5 V bucks from a 12 to 20 V input with a 0.5 s staggered start; an ESP32 supervisor drives each module's `PMIC_ENABLE` and buck enable, reads two INA3221 monitors on 20 mOhm shunts and has its own W5500 network identity (DeskPi wiki).
- Interconnect: the CM5 exposes MDI pairs only, so modules link through a switch with integrated 1000BASE-T PHYs, MDI to MDI without magnetics through a 0.1 uF capacitor in series on each of the eight signals (Microchip hardware design checklist DS00004151A, p. 12; the KSZ9897's voltage-mode drivers, DS00002330E section 7.2); magnetics only on the external uplink. Pick Microchip KSZ9897RTXI: five PHY and two MAC ports, 128-TQFP 0.4 mm, LCSC C638299 about USD 17, strap-configured unmanaged (Table 3-3), 2.5 W at full load on 1.2, 2.5 and 3.3 V rails, theta-JA 14.4 C/W on four layers, industrial grade wants six layers or forced air (section 6.2). The Realtek RTL8367S is USD 2.75 but its datasheet is NDA. PCIe between hosts needs a non-transparent bridge that does not exist here; USB host-to-host is not buildable with one OTG port per module.
- Sharing the radios: TMUX1308 (8:1 analogue mux, LCSC C970231, USD 0.19) per UART, SPI, I2S or I2C line; two cascaded TS3USB221 (USD 0.26 each, SCDS220M) for a USB pair; VBUS of a muxed USB device from a board rail. What a handover loses: nothing for GNSS and for GPIO expanders, a driver re-init for LoRa, an open SBD session for Iridium (keep the queue in replicated host storage), the PDP session for LTE, a USB re-enumeration for the SDR, an audio stream for the codec. DSI has no commodity mux; the display belongs to one node. Triplicating radios was weighed and rejected for the kit (three LoRa antennas on one channel in one case, one ZigBee coordinator per network).
- Supervisor: a small MCU (RP2040 or STM32G0) with its own watchdog and power domain; heartbeats on dedicated lines, per-module `PMIC_Enable` and buck enable, staggered start, one 2-bit owner bus that moves every muxed peripheral together, the fan, the INA3221s.
- Cluster software: k3s HA needs three servers with embedded etcd, and its requirements page says "etcd is write intensive; SD cards and eMMC cannot handle the IO load", so k3s means an NVMe per module (three M.2 2242 sites). Docker Swarm with three managers (Raft, quorum 2, ports 2377, 7946, 4789, fixed IPs) is light enough for eMMC and matches CubeOS ADR 0002. Neither shares a device across nodes: the owner runs the hardware service and the others reach it as a network service; placement by node label or a device-plugin resource, and a running task is not moved by a label change (`IgnoredDuringExecution`), so the supervisor's agent forces the move.
- Power and heat: three modules at the 2.5 A design point are 37.5 W plus about 3 W for the switch; four-node boards measure about 15 W per module at the wall under load (Geerling, NanoCluster); a sealed Peli case needs forced air; the CM5 datasheet publishes no thermal resistance, so the thermal design is empirical. Six Amphenol 4.0 mm connectors (LCSC C7435219) cost about USD 21.

## 5. CubeOS, its HAL and the bridge on three nodes (both repositories)

- The HAL is one privileged process per host (host network, pid host), discovers devices by a local sysfs walk and `/dev` globs (`ttyUSB*` and `ttyACM*` only), claims serial ports in an in-process table, carries no node identity, binds `0.0.0.0:6005` behind `X-HAL-Key` with three roles. It is a self-contained Go module (chi, go-systemd, dbus; no database, no broker, no import of the CubeOS API; a file-or-env ACL; a `CUBEOS_TIER=container` reduced mode) and can be lifted out unchanged.
- Nothing above it knows about more than one HAL: one `HAL_URL` in the API and in the bridge, every core stack pinned to `node.role == manager`, SQLite without replication on both sides (ADR 0003), k3s rejected (ADR 0002). The three-node kit is described in the CubeOS org profile README (node 1 reads the GPS on node 3 over HTTP) with "udev hotplug to Swarm labels" as an unchecked roadmap item; nothing of it exists in code.
- What already crosses the network: the bridge's Meshtastic and Iridium transports over the HAL, APRS over a KISS TCP host, MQTT, Reticulum, the hub uplink. Local by nature: cellular through ModemManager's D-Bus or raw AT, GPS NMEA, ZigBee ZNP, the SDR scanner, the panel expanders (timing), the DSI kiosk, UPS and watchdog.
- Live gaps for any Pi 5 or CM5 node: the HAL compose has `/dev/gpiochip4` commented out; the HAL does not scan `ttyAMA*`; the HAL and the bridge both auto-claim serial ports with only a manual `HAL_DISABLE_*` pair as arbitration.
- Verdict: reuse the HAL alone inside a MeshSat cluster stack (compose trimmed, the `meshsat` role widened to GPIO, I2C, USB, audio and Bluetooth, `gpiochip4` and `ttyAMA*` added, plus the capability-to-node registry that does not exist anywhere yet). CubeOS whole brings the AP, DHCP, Pi-hole, VPN and dashboard singletons and its SQLite into a cluster they were designed not to be in.
