# V2 red team, 9 September 2026

Written 9 September 2026, 02:40 to 03:20 CEST, against the repository at commit `00307c4` and the same
snapshot that went out as `meshsat-fieldkit-review-2026-09-09.zip`. Prompted by an external review whose
architecture-level points were largely right; every hardware claim below was re-derived from the generators
and the board files in this repository rather than taken from that review, and where I could not confirm a
claim I say so.

**Nothing here has been built.** These are defects in a design, found by reading it. No board has been
fabricated, so none of this has cost anything yet except the routing time already spent.

## Verdict

The architecture is credible and unusually well documented. The **electrical implementation has three defects
that would produce dead or dangerous hardware if the current gerbers were ordered**, and a fourth class of
problem where the specification claims behaviour the hardware does not implement. The routing and impedance
work is unfinished and known to be unfinished. My reading is the same as the external reviewer's in substance:

> detailed engineering prototype design, not fabrication-ready hardware.

I would add one thing the external review could not see from GitHub: **the repository's own gates did not
catch any of the three critical defects**, and that is the more important finding. Two of them are exactly the
kind of thing a gate can test cheaply, and the tests did not exist.

## Method

Read the six schematic generators (`gen_sch_{a,b,c,d,e,p}.py`), the five placement generators, the netlists
they produce, the routed board files of tonight's runs, and the DRC output. Traced the two safety lines
(EMCON, ZEROIZE) pin by pin across all four boards they touch. Checked every net class against the board
minimums. Compared the specification's numbers against the design record's own numbers. Where a claim needs a
vendor datasheet I do not have open, the finding says "verify" rather than asserting a failure.

## Critical

### C1. The hardware EMCON line is a ring oscillator, and its polarity is inverted between boards

This is the most serious finding in the design. EMCON is the one function the record insists must work without
Linux, without k3s and without firmware.

**What the panel defines** (`gen_sch_c.py:235`, `gen_sch_c.py:205`):

```
SW_EMCON pin 1 -> TX_INHIBIT_n, pin 2 -> GND        # closing the toggle pulls TX_INHIBIT_n to ground
R14 100k: TX_INHIBIT_n -> +3V3                      # pulled up when the toggle is open
U9 74LVC1G04: EMCON_HW = NOT TX_INHIBIT_n           # so EMCON asserted  =>  TX_INHIBIT_n low, EMCON_HW HIGH
```

**What the power board does with it** (`gen_sch_a.py:283`, a 74LVC08 quad AND):

```
1A = EMCON_HW, 1B = PA_SW_EN, 1Y = PA_EN            # PA_EN  = EMCON_HW AND PA_SW_EN
2A = EMCON_HW, 2B = HF_SW_EN, 2Y = HF_EN            # HF_EN  = EMCON_HW AND HF_SW_EN
3A = EMCON_HW, 3B = EMCON_HW, 3Y = TX_INHIBIT_n     # A22 DRIVES TX_INHIBIT_n from EMCON_HW
```

Three separate defects fall out of those six lines.

1. **A one-inversion feedback loop.** `TX_INHIBIT_n` goes to C7, is inverted into `EMCON_HW`, travels back to
   A22 over the ribbon, and A22's gate 3 drives `TX_INHIBIT_n` from it. One inverter in a closed loop with a
   push-pull driver is a ring oscillator. Its period is the loop delay, two gate delays plus two ribbon
   transits, so a few tens of nanoseconds: the EMCON line and every rail enable derived from it would toggle
   continuously, on a board that also carries a 30 W transmitter's keying.
2. **Output contention with the panel switch.** `SW_EMCON` is a mechanical contact to ground. A22's gate 3 is
   a push-pull CMOS output on the same net. Closing the toggle shorts a driven output to ground; the 74LVC08
   will survive it, but the design has two owners on a safety line and the DRC cannot see it because both are
   legal copper.
3. **The polarity is inverted.** C7 asserts EMCON by driving `EMCON_HW` **high**. A22 computes
   `PA_EN = EMCON_HW AND PA_SW_EN`, so `EMCON_HW` high **enables** the PA. The generator's own comment at
   `gen_sch_a.py:281` says it believes `EMCON_HW` is "active low from the panel controller ... pulled up: an
   unplugged ribbon leaves the transmitters enabled". Two boards hold opposite conventions for the same net,
   and the one on the power board also chooses the unsafe failure direction: a disconnected panel ribbon
   leaves the transmitters live.

The compute board has the same inversion in a different form (`gen_sch_b.py:291` and `:299`):

```
nfet(Q6, gate = +3V3, source = WIFI_W_DIS_n, drain = EMCON_HW)     # and the same for the 5G card
```

M.2 `W_DISABLE1#` is active low. With `EMCON_HW` low the FET pulls `W_DISABLE1#` down and the card is
disabled; with `EMCON_HW` high the card is enabled by its pull-up. So on B16 too, **asserting EMCON enables the
radios and releasing EMCON disables them.**

**Why no gate caught it.** `check_contracts.py:137` tests that `SW_EMCON` drives `TX_INHIBIT_n` and that
`EMCON_HW` appears on `J_PANEL`. It never asks how many devices drive a net, or in which sense. The check
passes on a design that does the opposite of what it says.

**Fix.** One owner per line: `TX_INHIBIT_n` is generated on C7 by the toggle alone and is an input everywhere
else; delete A22's U26 gate 3. Pick one sense and write it in `PANEL.md`: with the toggle closed the
transmitters are off, and every enable is `enable = SW_EN AND TX_INHIBIT_n` with `TX_INHIBIT_n` pulled **down**
on each consuming board so a disconnected ribbon inhibits rather than enables. Then add the contract tests the
existing one is missing: exactly one driver per safety net, and the polarity of each consumer.

### C2. The PoE port's positive feed passes through a 24.9 ohm 0603

`gen_sch_b.py:404`:

```
r("R13", "24.9 (POE_P tie)", "+54V_POE", "POE_P")
```

`POE_P` is the positive centre tap of the port magnetics (`gen_sch_b.py:391`, H5007NL pin 24), that is, the
power feed to the powered device. The negative side is switched correctly by the TPS23861's FET and its
0.255 ohm sense resistor. The positive side is not a sense path; it carries the port current.

At 802.3at (25.5 W at the device, about 600 mA at 54 V): 15 V dropped in R13, leaving about 39 V at the port
against the 44 V minimum, and **9 W dissipated in an 0603 rated for 0.1 W**. At 802.3af it is still 8.7 V and
3 W. The port cannot work and the resistor fails immediately.

The inline comment says the value is "for the fit of the PSE detection". Detection and classification are done
by the TPS23861 on its own pins; nothing in the reference design puts a resistor in the feed.

**Fix.** Connect `+54V_POE` to the centre tap directly, or fit a 0 ohm link if a place to open the path is
wanted. Add a gate: any resistor above a few tens of milliohms in a net that a rail declares as a power path
is an error.

### C3. Net class clearances below the board minimum (found and fixed tonight)

`gen_pcb_a3.py` and `gen_pcb_b3.py` declared a USB class clearance of 0.10 mm on boards whose
`m_MinClearance` is 0.127 mm. KiCad enforces the board minimum as a floor; Freerouting takes the class value
from the DSN and lays to it. **A23's route came back with 25 clearance violations**, every one reading
`board minimum clearance 0.1270 mm; actual 0.1017 mm` and every one between the two legs of a pair.

Fixed on 9 September (commit `4ffa0b6`): both classes raised to 0.127 with the pair gap at 0.13, and the
placement generators now refuse to run when any class clearance is below the board minimum. Recorded in
appendix 32.79. Listed here as Critical because it was in the gerbers a reviewer would have been given.

## High

### H1. ZEROIZE has no hardware path at all

The specification says the panel carries "the hardware EMCON and ZEROIZE logic independent of any module" and
the toggle's description says "ZEROIZE_HW low = wipe the secure element and assert the disk-key wipe".

What the hardware does: `SW_ZERO` pulls `ZEROIZE_HW` to ground; it is pulled up twice (on C7 and again by
`R117` on A22, `gen_sch_a.py:304`); the panel RP2040 reads it on GPIO 22; it is carried to every ribbon and to
a test point on D9. **Nothing acts on it.** The ATECC608B (`gen_sch_b.py:482`) has exactly four connections,
`SDA`, `SCL`, `+3V3_DEV` and `GND`: no reset, no gated supply, no connection to `ZEROIZE_HW`.

So ZEROIZE is a switch that a microcontroller reads, which is precisely what the record says EMCON must not
be. Worse, the mechanism named cannot be implemented as stated: once an ATECC608B's slots are locked its keys
are not erasable, so "wipe the secure element" needs either a key-encryption-key held somewhere erasable or
physical destruction of the part.

**Fix.** Decide what ZEROIZE means, then implement it: at minimum a hardware gate on the secure element's
supply and on the NVMe drives' power, plus a documented key hierarchy where the erasable item is a KEK in a
volatile or writable store. If it stays software-mediated, say so in the specification and stop calling it
hardware.

### H2. Every PCIe lane is designed and judged to the wrong impedance

`gen_pcb_b3.py:258` assigns `PCIE*`, `NVME*_RX/TX/CLK*` and `CARD*_RX/TX/CLK*` to the **USB** class, whose
intent target is 90 ohm and whose geometry computes 94 ohm. PCIe and M.2 require **85 ohm** differential.
Separately, `intent.py:12` defines a `PCIE` class at **100 ohm**, which is also wrong, and which is never used
because no net is assigned to it.

94 ohm against an 85 ohm requirement is +10.6 percent, inside the CEM specification's plus or minus 15 percent
window but at its edge, with no margin for the etch tolerance the boards will actually be built to. The point
is not that it certainly fails: the point is that the judge is checking the wrong number, so it would never be
reported either way. The reference clock pairs need their own 100 ohm class.

### H3. Differential pairs on the routed boards are mostly uncoupled

Freerouting 1.9.0 has no differential-pair router. The pre-router lays pairs as locked, coupled copper before
the route, and whatever it cannot lay is routed as two independent traces. Tonight's measurements:

| Board | pairs pre-laid | what the judge says |
|---|---|---|
| D9 | 4 of 4 USB | three at 89 ohm, one SHORT: released |
| C8 | 1 of 1 | 73 mm of 1014 mm on the net; the rest is the router's, legs 3.03 mm apart, 110 ohm, UNCOUPLED |
| A23 | 1 of 3 | the other two are the router's |
| B17 | 36 of 99 | the remaining 63 are the router's |

`impedance_check.py` returns non-zero if any pair misses, so **no board with a router-laid pair can pass the
gate as it stands**. That is the binding constraint on the whole set and it is an owner decision, written up
in appendix 32.76: teach the pre-router to lay a whole net, record a measured exception per pair, or judge the
controlled fraction rather than all of it.

### H4. Gigabit Ethernet between the CM5s and the switch is transformerless, with 100 nF coupling

`gen_sch_b.py:358` puts a 100 nF 0402 in series with each leg of each of the four MDI pairs of each slot,
between the CM5's on-module PHY and the KSZ9897R's integrated PHY. There are no magnetics on those three
links (the H5007NL serves the wall port only) and I see no common-mode termination.

Capacitively coupled PHY-to-PHY 1000BASE-T is done in practice, but it is chipset-specific: both PHYs must
tolerate the loss of the transformer's DC path and common-mode rejection, and the vendors document the
allowed topology. **Verify against the KSZ9897R datasheet and the CM5's PHY (BCM54213PE) before fabrication.**
If either vendor requires magnetics, three links need rework, which is a board change.

I flag this as a risk to check, not as a proven error: this is the one electrical item in the report I could
not settle from the repository alone.

### H5. No decoupling capacitor on any board is within 3 mm of the pin it serves

Now that each generator declares which capacitor serves which pin, the gate measures it for the first time:

| Board | declared entries | within 3 mm |
|---|---|---|
| A | 30 | 0 (worst: C106 and C107 at 117 and 121 mm from U26 pin 14) |
| B | 30 | 0 |
| C | 18 | 0 |
| D | 22 | 0 (8.2 to 32.1 mm) |
| E | 16 | not yet run on a routed board |
| P | 3 | not yet run on a routed board |

The cause is one line of method: every placement generator shelf-packs passives into region rectangles in
reference order, so a capacitor lands wherever the packer reached. A 100 nF at 10 mm has roughly 10 to 15 nH
of loop inductance against 1 to 2 nH at 1 mm, so the local bypass stops working above a few megahertz. The
rails still have bulk capacitance at the regulators and the boards have solid ground planes, so nothing is
dead; what is missing is the decoupling the parts' own datasheets ask for, on a kit whose test plan includes
RE102 and CS114. Options and costs are in appendix 32.74. **Owner decision, open.**

### H6. Compute redundancy is real; peripheral redundancy is not

The specification's requirement line says "no single CM5 is a single point of failure ... every device visible
to all modules". The Devices line, four rows later, says "the physical owner is a cabling choice recorded in
the HAL configuration". Both are true, and they mean different things. A USB device belongs to one slot's hub.
If that CM5 fails, k3s moves the workload; **it does not move the USB device**, because there is no multi-host
USB switching in the design. The bearer attached to the dead slot is gone until someone re-cables.

This is not necessarily wrong as a design, but the requirement line as written is not what the hardware does.
The specification should say: compute is redundant, peripheral paths are not, and here is the per-bearer table
of which slot owns what and what is lost when that slot dies.

### H7. The k3s claims do not hold

Two errors in the same row (`V2-SPEC.md:31` and `:29`):

- "storage replicated across slots by k3s": k3s does not replicate storage. Its default `local-path` provisioner
  is node-local. Replication needs Longhorn, Ceph or equivalent, which is a real resource decision on three
  8 GB modules.
- A control plane on one server with two agents is not highly available. Embedded etcd needs three servers for
  quorum. The repository does not actually specify the server and agent split anywhere I can find, which is
  itself the gap: the one architectural claim the whole redundancy story rests on is unwritten.

### H8. The energy, power and runtime numbers do not close

`V2-SPEC.md:20` and `:23` say a 200 Wh pack and "about 8 to 9.5 h typical (29 W)". 200 Wh at 29 W is
**6.9 hours** at 100 percent usable energy, and a real 4S pack with its protection and gauge gives 85 to 90
percent, so 5.9 to 6.2 hours. To reach 8 to 9.5 hours at 29 W needs 232 to 276 Wh usable.

Separately, the specification's budget (29 W typical, 150 W peak) contradicts the design record's own budget
in appendix 32.52 (**50 W typical, 200 W peak, 290 W with the outlets**), which is what the fuses, the dock
pins and the pack were sized from. At 50 W the same pack gives 3.4 to 4 hours.

One of these numbers came from an earlier revision. Until it is settled, no endurance figure in the
specification should be quoted to anyone.

### H9. Thermal is the largest untested physical risk

A sealed case with no vent anywhere (owner ruling, 7 September), containing three CM5s, three NVMe drives,
three PCIe switches, three USB hubs, a 5G module, a WiFi card, a LimeSDR, a monitor, the DC/DC stages and a
30 W VHF PA, with five internal fans moving heat to the aluminium plate and the walls. The record's estimate
is +10 K with one module and +16 K with three loaded, lid open. That is an estimate against an enclosure model,
not a measurement.

The first test to run on the first prototype is the one the test plan already contains: **10 minutes of 30 W
key-down with the case closed**, instrumented at the PA flange, the plate, the CM5 cores and the pack. If that
one fails, several other decisions change.

### H10. RF self-compatibility is untested and the enclosure is small

Eleven simultaneous RF paths in one sealed aluminium box: a 30 W VHF PA, a 5 W HF transmitter, a 1 W LoRa PA, a
5G module, two WiFi radios, two 2.4 GHz mesh radios, an Iridium transceiver, a multi-band GNSS receiver and a
wideband SDR. The design anticipates this correctly (arrestors, an SDR limiter, EMCON, a self-compatibility
test in the plan), but none of it has run, and C1 means the one hardware mitigation that exists today is
wired backwards.

Measurements I would put first: VHF TX into GNSS desense, VHF TX into the SDR front end, HF TX common-mode
ingress into USB and HDMI, 5G TX into GNSS, and WiFi against the two 2.4 GHz mesh radios.

## Medium

- **M1. LoRa breaks the "every radio is a USB device" rule.** The E22-900M30S sits on slot 3's SPI
  (`gen_sch_b.py:13`, `:226`: "the SPI0 breakout exists on S3 only"). It is the one bearer with a hard slot
  affinity. Either accept and document it, or put it behind a USB bridge like the other UART radios.
- **M2. The single points of failure are not the CM5s.** A22 carries every rail; C7 plus one 26-way ribbon
  carries EMCON, ZEROIZE, the kill line and the heartbeats; the KSZ9897R is the entire cluster fabric; E5 and
  E6 are the only way power enters. Losing any one of them loses the kit. That is a legitimate design choice
  for a prototype, but the redundancy story should say it.
- **M3. Two RP2040 supply pins on C have no capacitor of their own**: ADC_AVDD (pin 43, and `RAIL_SENSE` is an
  ADC input) and USB_VDD (pin 48) share the seven 100 nF that serve the six IOVDD pins and the flash.
- **M4. A23 routes badly under its own power copper.** 18 passes in 3 h 29 min left 41 nets unrouted before
  the C3 fix. The band keep-outs that carry the 8 A rails constrain the router hard; the trade between copper
  and routability has not been measured.
- **M5. B17 loses 34 to 41 pads' escapes** at the pre-route, of which 6 to 16 are caused by another part's pad
  (a placement fault) and the rest by copper this pipeline lays itself.
- **M6. `nfet()` has three different argument orders** in `gen_sch_a.py` (ref, value, gate, drain, source),
  `gen_sch_b.py` and `gen_sch_d.py` (ref, gate, source, drain, value). The A19 Q5 source and drain swap of
  4 September came from exactly this class of confusion, and nothing prevents the next one.
- **M7. The gates do not test what they claim.** `check_contracts.py` verifies that a net exists on both sides
  of a connector, never its direction, its polarity or how many devices drive it. `place_audit.py` blocks on
  hand-set thresholds. Three gates had never executed at all until 8 September. The rule the record now
  carries, that a gate which has never printed a non-trivial denominator has never run, should be extended:
  a gate that cannot fail on a known-bad input has not been tested.
- **M8. The printed review sheets are one phase behind the board folders** (D8 and P1 against D9 and P2). The
  handoff that rebuilds them has not run since the wave started.
- **M9. The 5G module and its jack count are still open** in the specification's own words, on a board whose
  M.2 B-key site and two east jacks are already drawn.
- **M10. Procurability is unproven**: 79 of B16's 173 BOM lines had no LCSC code at quote time, and the
  quote-only folder exists precisely because that board is not routed.

## Low and process

- **P1.** A pass ceiling set from ambition rather than from a measured pass rate cost A23 two rounds (2 h and
  4 h) that wrote nothing, because Freerouting 1.9.0 saves its session only when the whole job ends. Rule
  adopted: a 3-pass probe first, ceiling from the measurement.
- **P2.** A debug print that did not apply the same exemption as the check it explained sent an evening into a
  wrong theory about escape depths. Fixed; the real cause was a straight tail run drawn across a fine-pitch fan.
- **P3.** `escape_prune.py` was deleting 34 mm pre-routed differential legs to make a DRC count go down. Now it
  removes the shortest item in a violation and refuses to touch two long laid runs.
- **P4.** Every placement generator stamped the previous phase on the silk. P2 shipped stamped P1.
- **P5.** The sounder driver sat 294 mm from the sounder, which is why one net could not be routed at all.

## Where I differ from the external review

- Its architecture reading is correct, including the compute-versus-peripheral distinction, the LoRa
  exception, the k3s errors and the energy arithmetic. I confirmed each independently.
- Its three specific hardware claims are real: I confirmed the EMCON defect (and found it is worse than
  stated, a feedback loop and a contention as well as an inversion), the 24.9 ohm PoE resistor, and the
  ZEROIZE-to-ATECC608B gap.
- Its priority list is close to mine with one change: **C1 outranks everything**, including B17. A board that
  routes perfectly and gates its 30 W transmitter backwards is worse than a board that does not route.
- One claim I could not confirm: the review says the specification calls for "one K3s server plus two agents".
  I cannot find that split anywhere in the repository. The specification says only "k3s across the three
  modules". The absence is the finding.

## What I would do next, in order

1. **Fix C1 and C2 in the generators** and regenerate. Both are single-figure edits. Then add the two contract
   tests that would have caught them: one driver per safety net, and the declared polarity of every consumer.
2. **Decide what ZEROIZE is** (H1) and either implement it in hardware or restate it in the specification.
3. **Give PCIe its own 85 ohm class** (H2) and fix the `PCIE` entry in `intent.py`.
4. **Settle the pair question** (H3). It blocks every board release.
5. **Settle the numbers** (H8): one power budget, one pack energy, one runtime, everywhere.
6. **Check the transformerless Ethernet against both PHY datasheets** (H4) before any B17 order.
7. Only then the thermal and RF campaigns (H9, H10), which need a built prototype.

The decoupling placement (H5) is the one item that is already written up as an owner decision with its costs,
in appendix 32.74, and it does not block any of the above.

# Annex A: security red team, 9 September 2026 03:40 CEST

A second external review looked at the same pack from a security and provisioning angle. I checked every one
of its claims against this repository. **All ten hold**, one of them was already fixed while I was checking,
and one sentence in its summary needs correcting in the design's favour and then against it.

## Confirmed, with the evidence I found

| # | Finding | What I verified |
|---|---|---|
| S1 | **ZEROIZE is not software-independent and fails open** | `PANEL.md:34` calls pin 10 "out, hardware: low = wipe"; `PANEL.md:112` says the secure element and the encrypted drives "are wiped **by the supervisor on the falling edge**". The line is pulled up on C7 and again by `R117` on A22, so a removed panel or a cut ribbon reads high, which is "no wipe". A compromised or hung supervisor simply does not act. This is the same defect as H1 in the main report, seen from the attacker's side |
| S2 | **No secure boot requirement, while flashing is deliberately exposed** | B16 carries `J_FLASH1..3` (three USB-C receptacles), `J_RPIBOOT1..3` ("fit to flash the eMMC") and `J_DBG1..3` ("console UART0 and the module I2C, 3.3 V, bench") at `gen_sch_b.py:345,347,349`. Nothing in `V2-SPEC.md` or `BUILD.md` mandates verified boot, a signing key in OTP or a boot-order restriction. Encrypted drives without secure boot protect data at rest from a stolen kit, not from an attacker who reflashes the module |
| S3 | **The pack gauge has no provisioning step** | `BUILD.md:66` describes the pack build and the charger settings and says nothing about the BQ4050's keys or sealing. The part ships in full access with documented default keys, so an unsealed pack exposes its protection thresholds over the SMBus pair that leaves the board on `J_SMB` |
| S4 | **Both trust controllers are reflashable by hand** | C7 has `JP1` ("BOOTSEL: short while powering to enter the USB bootloader") and `TP1`/`TP2` on SWCLK and SWDIO; E6 has the same jumper. These two RP2040s hold the EMCON and ZEROIZE logic and the charge and sensor decisions |
| S5 | **The wall Ethernet port shares the switch with the cluster** | `gen_sch_b.py:12`: KSZ9897R "ports 1-3 the modules, port 4 the wall RJ45". No VLAN, ACL or 802.1X policy is written anywhere in the pack. The part supports all three, so this is unspecified rather than impossible |
| S6 | **The wall USB port is a host port into a privileged node** | By design (`V2-SPEC.md`, the A22 wall channel). No device-class or authorization policy is documented |
| S7 | **The deliverable BOMs carried wrong part codes** | Verified and **fixed today**, see below |
| S8 | **B16 cannot be signed off from this pack** | It is placed and unrouted, stamped `QUOTE-ONLY.txt`, and it holds the CM5s, the secure element, the Ethernet fabric and most of the external attack surface |
| S9 | **Safety nets carry locally overridden DRC warnings** | A22's report has `[track_dangling] Local override; warning` on a 2.34 mm `/EMCON_HW` track; C7's has `[via_dangling] Local override; warning` on a `/ZEROIZE_HW` via. Neither necessarily breaks the net, and both are on the two nets where "probably fine" is not good enough |
| S10 | **External low-level buses are trusted** | E6 takes the outside sensor pod's I2C and the pack's SMBus. Battery telemetry feeds charge decisions, and a stuck or hostile device on either bus is not handled anywhere in the design |

## One correction to that review, and it makes things worse

Its summary says "EMCON fails safe; ZEROIZE fails open", and recommends using EMCON as the model for
rebuilding ZEROIZE.

**The EMCON architecture is the right model. The EMCON implementation is not, and it does not fail safe.**
Finding C1 of the main report: A22's own comment at `gen_sch_a.py:281` says the line is pulled up so that "an
unplugged ribbon leaves the transmitters enabled", A22 computes `PA_EN = EMCON_HW AND PA_SW_EN` so asserting
EMCON enables the PA, B16 releases both M.2 radios' `W_DISABLE1#` when EMCON is asserted, and A22 drives
`TX_INHIBIT_n` from `EMCON_HW` while C7 derives `EMCON_HW` by inverting `TX_INHIBIT_n`, which closes a
one-inversion loop. So both safety lines fail open today, and one of them oscillates. The reviewer could not
see this because it needs the netlists of four boards at once, not the documents.

That does not change their recommendation, it strengthens it: build one trust domain that owns both lines,
with a single driver, a stated sense, pull-downs on every consumer so a missing panel inhibits, and a gate
that tests the sense rather than the presence of a net.

## Fixed while checking (S7)

The claim was that the shipped BOMs still carry codes the project's own record says are wrong. It is worse
than the review could see from the file it quoted. I compared every deliverable BOM against the order set that
the ordering session verified on JLCPCB's parts API on 8 September:

| Deliverable | wrong codes | examples |
|---|---|---|
| A22 | 3 | `C5605` for the 74LVC08 EMCON gate (it is a 74HC14D), `C5626` twice for the PCA9555 (it is a 74HC245PW) |
| B16 quote | 38 | the M.2 socket and LimeSDR connector codes, the LED |
| C7 | 5 | `C2166` for the BAT54 (it is an LED), `C50993` twice for the PCA9555 (not in the catalogue), three resistors of the wrong package |
| E6 | 1 | the green LED with one piece in stock |
| **P2** | 2 | `C2825431` for the CSD17570Q5B pack FETs (it is a lever switch) |

P2 matters most: it was **released on 8 September, after the codes were corrected**, and it still shipped the
wrong one. The reason is structural and is now written down: a finish re-exports the BOM from the board file it
already has and never re-runs the schematic generator, so a code corrected in a generator does not reach a
deliverable until that whole board is regenerated from its schematic.

## What I would add to their fix list

1. **One trust domain, both lines.** Their point 1 is right and should cover EMCON as well as ZEROIZE, because
   EMCON is broken in the same way. A small dedicated controller or discrete logic that owns `TX_INHIBIT_n` and
   `ZEROIZE_HW`, drives the transmitter gates and the erase path directly, and is not reachable from any CM5.
2. **Say what becomes irrecoverable.** "Wipe the secure element" is not implementable on a locked ATECC608B.
   Define the key hierarchy first: a device-bound KEK in something erasable, the drives' LUKS secret wrapped by
   it, and ZEROIZE erases the KEK. Then the hardware requirement follows from it.
3. **Make the flashing and debug surface a build-time state.** Three USB-C flashing ports, three RPIBOOT
   jumpers, three console headers and two BOOTSEL jumpers are the right thing to have on a prototype and the
   wrong thing to ship. Decide which are depopulated, which are behind the sealed case only, and which stay.
4. **Treat the wall RJ45 and the wall USB as hostile.** Port 4 of the KSZ9897R in its own VLAN with ACLs, and a
   USB authorization policy on whichever slot owns the wall port.
5. **A hostile-interface test section in `TEST-PLAN.md`**, as they propose. I would add one case to their list:
   assert EMCON and ZEROIZE with the panel ribbon unplugged, and with it cut mid-operation, and record what the
   transmitters and the keys do.
