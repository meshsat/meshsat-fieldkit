## Scope, rule and roles

**Scope.** The Layer 4 tasks L4-E7 (board E's solar stage settings), L4-E7Q (the qualification of L4-E7's 100 W bound),
L4-E7R (the control decision for REQ-016's 100 W), L4-E8 (board A's VBUS20 bank), L4-E9 (the connected power architecture
and its closure gate), L4-E10 (FEA-008, the cell and thermal design), L4-E11 (U-04, D-06 and D-09), L4-E12 (U-02, the
electronics against the inside air) and L4-E13 (U-03, the panel). Every blocking item of every collaborator check of those
tasks has one row: the first check's items and the recheck's items. A recheck item that restates a first-check item has its
own row, and the two rows point at each other. `findings_inventory.py` beside this page lists the 58 items from the check
files; `v2/ecad/tools/tests/test_l4close.py` holds the rows to that list.

**The owner's rule (item 3, 2 October 2026), in his words:** "For the final candidates, map every previously rejected
material finding to its correction and targeted verification. Identify the reviewer and exact revision. Preserve Astra's
original verdicts. Passing software tests supports the calculations and tooling; engineering acceptance also needs the
assumptions, bounds and physical reasoning to hold. Exhausting the review allowance does not itself close a finding. Use
the existing checks wherever sufficient. Add verification only for a concrete remaining risk."

**The reviewers.**
- **The engineering collaborator, Astra** (model gpt-6-astra at effort xhigh, run by the launcher as a read-only job): one
  focused check and one targeted recheck per issue. Its files are filed as returned. Their first line (`accepted: yes` or
  `accepted: no`) and their summary verdict are quoted below unchanged; a NOT YET is never restated as accepted.
- **The coordinator (Claude):** the closing checks `checks/check-<task>-N.md`. They are not model reviews and not Astra
  checks. Each row says what such a check recomputed in separate arithmetic or read at the source, and says plainly where it
  only re-ran the record's script (byte for byte) and the tests.
- **The authors' rounds:** the commits named in each row, on the task's branch, all reachable from `aa897e38`.

**What counts as a targeted verification here.** An Astra recheck item that read the correction (its verdict quoted), or a
coordinator check that names the item and states what it recomputed or read at the source. A byte-for-byte reproduction
of a script's output and a passing test show that the script, the record and the tests agree: they support the calculation
and the tooling, not the assumption, bound or physical reasoning under it. That both Astra runs on an issue are spent
closes nothing by itself.

**The four residual states.**
- **CLOSED:** the correction holds on its evidence, nothing the finding asked for is outstanding, and a targeted
  verification read it.
- **CLOSED AS CONDITIONAL:** the finding objected to an unsupported claim; the record now states the result as CONDITIONAL
  with its named dependency (and the register row or owner item that carries it) and its fallback where the record names
  one, and a targeted verification read that statement. A correct statement, not a resolution.
- **OPEN DOWNSTREAM:** what the finding asked for includes evidence, a measurement or an analysis that the record assigned to
  a row of `v2/docs/records/l4e9/DOWNSTREAM-REGISTER.md` with one owner and an acceptance (the rows are named). The
  assignment is closed, the item is not.
- **STILL OPEN:** no correction, or a correction that no targeted verification has read. The row says exactly what is
  missing, and the section "Concrete remaining risks" gives the smallest verification that would settle it.

A first-check row whose remainder the recheck restated takes the least closed state of the rows it points to (in the
order STILL OPEN, OPEN DOWNSTREAM, CLOSED AS CONDITIONAL, CLOSED), so a finding is never read as closed in one row and open
in the other.
