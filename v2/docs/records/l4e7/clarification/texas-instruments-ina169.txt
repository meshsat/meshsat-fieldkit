Subject: INA169NA: the VIN+ pin current, and the reading of the CMR and PSR rows at VSENSE = 50 mV

We use the INA169NA (data sheet SBOS181F, December 2000, revised February 2017) as a high-side monitor on a solar
converter's input, 9 V to 25 V common mode, V+ tied to the shunt's load side, its output current into 25 kOhm to ground,
the air at -20 C to about +62 C. A comparator trips on the output at a sense voltage of about 50 mV. Our bound uses
the Electrical Characteristics on page 6: the transconductance (990 to 1010 uA/V), the offset (1 mV), the nonlinearity
(0.1 %), and the common-mode rejection (100 dB minimum, VIN+ = 2.7 V to 60 V) and supply rejection (10 uV/V maximum,
V+ = 2.7 V to 60 V), both printed at VSENSE = 50 mV.

1. The input bias current is printed as 10 uA typical. What maximum current do you warrant into the VIN+ pin, its
   output current included, over -40 to 85 C and VIN+ up to 25 V? Meanwhile we carry it at the pin's absolute maximum
   input current, 10 mA (page 4).
2. Are the CMR and PSR rows measured as the change of the whole output at VSENSE = 50 mV, so that they include any change
   of the transconductance with the common-mode and supply voltages? We read them so.

For each item we ask for a limit Texas Instruments warrants, and the conditions under which it applies. Characterization
over production lots is welcome as supporting evidence, but on its own we cannot treat it as a production guarantee. The
answers are supporting: our bound does not wait on them. Thank you.
