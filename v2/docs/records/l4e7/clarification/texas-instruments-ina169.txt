Subject: INA169NA: a warranted error envelope at V+ = VIN+ of 9 V to 25 V and VSENSE of 46 mV to 53 mV, and the VIN+ current

We use the INA169NA (data sheet SBOS181F, December 2000, revised February 2017) as a high-side monitor on a solar
converter's input: VIN+ and V+ both at 9 V to 25 V (V+ tied to the shunt's load side, so V+ = VIN+ - VSENSE), its output
current into 25.15 kOhm to ground (16.9k over 8.25k, a comparator on the 8.25k), the air at -20 C to about +62 C. The
comparator trips at a sense voltage between about 46 mV and 53 mV over the parts' tolerances and life. Our bound uses the
Electrical Characteristics on page 6: the transconductance (990 to 1010 uA/V, VSENSE 10 mV to 150 mV), the offset (1 mV),
the nonlinearity (0.1 %), all at V+ = 5 V, VIN+ = 12 V and ROUT = 25 kOhm, and the common-mode rejection (100 dB minimum,
VIN+ = 2.7 V to 60 V) and supply rejection (10 uV/V maximum, V+ = 2.7 V to 60 V), both printed at VSENSE = 50 mV.

1. What total error of the output current do you warrant, gain and offset together, over V+ = VIN+ = 9 V to 25 V, VSENSE
   = 46 mV to 53 mV, ROUT = 25 kOhm and TA = -40 C to 85 C? We read the CMR and PSR rows as the change of the whole
   output at VSENSE = 50 mV. Meanwhile we carry, as our own assumption, a change of the transconductance with V+ and VIN+
   of at most 1 % (its effect at our 51.4 mV highest trip is 13.5 uV referred to the input); our bound reaches its limit only if
   that change reaches about 100 %.
2. The input bias current is printed as 10 uA typical. What maximum current do you warrant into the VIN+ pin, its output
   current included, over -40 C to 85 C and VIN+ up to 25 V? Meanwhile we carry 1 mA as our own assumption; our bound
   reaches its limit only at about 100 mA, above the pin's 10 mA absolute maximum (page 4).
3. During a discharge or a surge the sense voltage may reach about 0.92 V for tens of nanoseconds and 0.54 V for about a
   millisecond (under the 2 V differential absolute maximum). Is the output current then still the sense voltage over the
   internal 1 kOhm, or is it limited, and is there any condition under which it exceeds that?

For each item we ask for a limit Texas Instruments warrants, and the conditions under which it applies. Characterization
over production lots is welcome as supporting evidence, but on its own we cannot treat it as a production guarantee. The
answers are supporting: our bound does not wait on them. Thank you.
