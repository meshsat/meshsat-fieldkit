Subject: SOLBIANFLEX SX 156: the production tolerance of the open-circuit voltage at STC

We are evaluating one SX 156 as the solar input of a portable unit whose charger accepts a panel only if its open-circuit
voltage stays at or below 25.0 V with the cells at -20 C, and holds the panel at 16.4 to 18.8 V. Your SX series datasheet
(English, February 2023) gives the SX 156 as 156 W, Vmp 17.6 V, Imp 8.9 A, Voc 21.3 V, Isc 9.4 A at STC, NOCT 45 +- 2 C,
a Voc temperature coefficient of -0.32 %/C, and a positive power tolerance of (0%, +5%). It gives no tolerance for Voc. On
the typical values the open-circuit voltage at -20 C is 24.37 V, so a unit more than 2.6 % above the typical Voc would
exceed our limit.

1. What tolerance on the open-circuit voltage at STC do you warrant for production SX 156 panels (for example the
   flash-test acceptance band, or the tolerance printed on the nameplate), above and below 21.3 V?
2. Is the -0.32 %/C coefficient a typical value? What limits apply to it, and over which temperature range (down to
   -40 C)?
3. Is the SX 156 of the February 2023 sheet the current model? An earlier sheet listed an SX 158 with the same Voc and a
   Vmp of 17.4 V: which applies to panels shipped today?
4. Can a flash-test report (Voc, Isc, Vmp, Imp at STC) be supplied for each panel by serial number with the delivery?
5. If no tolerance is part of the specification, can you provide the distribution of Voc at STC over production lots (the
   sample size and the number of lots)? We would take it as supporting evidence only.

What we need is a tolerance you warrant on Voc at STC for the panels shipped, and the conditions under which it applies;
characterization data alone we cannot treat as a production guarantee. A warranted Voc at STC between 20.10 V and 21.85 V
would meet both of our limits. Thank you.
