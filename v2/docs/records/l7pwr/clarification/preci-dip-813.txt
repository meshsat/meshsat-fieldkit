Subject: 813-S1-012-10-016101 (813 series spring-loaded connector, 2.54 mm, double row, 12 contacts, initial height 7 mm):
the current-time capability of one contact

We are designing a prototype field unit in which one contact of an 813-S1-012-10-016101 (your "Spring-Loaded Connectors"
catalogue, page 34: operating current max. 3.5 A, contact resistance 10 mOhm static at the halfway position, music-wire spring
gold plated, -55 to +85 C with the music-wire spring, page 31) carries a 9.5 to 17.4 V supply of 1.0 A continuous (up to 1.3 A
with the fans at full speed) from a carrier board to a docking board. The supply is protected upstream by an electronic fuse
(Texas Instruments TPS16630) whose printed behaviour sets the fault duties below. The contact may reach 85 C body temperature in
service (the inside air of a sealed enclosure). Seven further contacts of the same connector carry the return.

Could you tell us, for this contact (brass piston and barrel, gold-plated music-wire spring, 0.25 um gold on the barrel and
0.5 um on the piston):

1. Its non-repetitive pulse capability. The eFuse's fast trip bounds a hard short applied while the supply is on to at most
   566 A for at most 4.5 microseconds (at most 1.44 A2s), once per event, through the contact at its halfway position. Is a peak
   current or an I2t figure for a single pulse of about 5 us available, and what is the failure mode you would expect first (the
   piston-to-barrel interface, the plating, the spring if it carries current)? Your surface-mount series page prints "Max. 3.5 A /
   7A peak"; is a peak figure defined for the 813 solder-tail series, and over what duration is "peak" defined?

2. Its repetitive duty capability. After a start into a persistent short the eFuse limits the current to 1.47 to 1.80 A for at
   most 1.5 s, switches off for 0.5 to 0.8 s and retries, so the contact can see 1.80 A at a duty of at most 0.75 for as long
   as the short persists (we test one hour at a 70 C ambient). Is this inside the 3.5 A operating rating at 85 C body
   temperature, and is the 3.5 A rating itself valid up to the music-wire spring's 85 C, or derated with temperature?

3. The contact resistance criterion. Our acceptance after each fault case is a four-wire reading at 1 A within +10 % of the
   contact's first reading and at most 20 mOhm. Is the catalogue's 10 mOhm a maximum or a typical figure, at what current is it
   measured, and what change of contact resistance after a current event would you regard as damage?

4. Whether the spring is in the current path at the halfway position, or the piston contacts the barrel wall so that the
   spring carries no appreciable current.

5. The plating specification of page 178 of the full catalogue, which the SLC catalogue refers to, for the S1 code.

A current-time curve, an I2t figure or a statement of the test you qualify the contacts with would let us close the question
without a bench test on your parts. Thank you.
